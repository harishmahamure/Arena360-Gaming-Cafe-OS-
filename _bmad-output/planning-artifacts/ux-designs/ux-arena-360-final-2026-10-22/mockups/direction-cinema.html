Cinema

Premium and cinematic — full-bleed key art, frosted-glass panels, light thin type. The PC feels like a console dashboard.

Treats each kiosk like a premium console home screen: the hero art fills the whole 1080p canvas, UI floats on frosted glass, the title is huge but thin, and the featured row sits at the bottom like a media shelf. A warm champagne accent signals 'premium lounge' rather than 'LAN party'. The Hub keeps the deep-navy glass look but stays solid underneath for legibility — glass is decoration, not structure.

  • Strengths: Highest perceived value — supports premium pricing and upscale branches; showcases licensed art; feels modern next to Steam Big Picture / Xbox UI
  • Weaknesses: WPF has no cheap live backdrop blur — needs pre-blurred art per hero or BlurEffect (GPU cost on GTX 1050-class/older PCs); text contrast depends on whatever art is behind it; thin type at small sizes hurts readability
  • Cheap monitors: Weakest of the four — translucent panels and thin weights lose contrast on low-gamut TN panels; Hub glass is a staff-fatigue risk unless kept subtle
